# Council invitations and the FlexZone introduction

Status: chosen experience direction, approved in discussion. Production sponsorship, records access, and verification arrangements remain unconfirmed.

## Positioning

> Your council is offering you an opportunity to help make room, and earn something for your flexibility.

FlexPay begins as a council-led invitation to participate in a local pilot. The invitation is linked to a vehicle by an authorised records partner. The recipient chooses whether to activate an account and participate.

Prepare an invitation record rather than automatically enrolling the person in an active rewards account. This keeps joining and any subsequent offer acceptance explicit.

The council-led direction is a service-design hypothesis about relevance, trust, and reduced onboarding effort. It does not demonstrate public acceptance or that a specific council can operate the selected road pilot.

## The FlexZone

A FlexZone is a clearly bounded section of road. Each offer specifies a direction, date, and time window. Show the road boundaries on a small map and the purchased window on a timeline, keeping both consistent through the offer journey.

Use “make room” as the shared purpose and concrete road/window language for the exchange. Describe pressure on the road rather than labelling the person as being in a pressure zone. Eligibility concerns relevant use of the road; living nearby is not sufficient.

Do not promise a specific journey-time improvement without model evidence or suggest that FlexPay knows the person’s current location.

## Illustrative letter

> A little room on your morning route.
>
> [Council] is inviting you to join a FlexPay pilot for [road section].
>
> When this stretch needs some breathing room, you could earn rewards for changing when or how you travel.
>
> Your invitation is linked to vehicle [registration]. Activate it to explore your FlexZone and see how participating could work for you.

Include an official entry address, a QR code, and a unique invitation code. Provide an accessible alternative for people who cannot use the QR code. Explain the source of the vehicle link and what activation authorises.

The sponsor, road, registration, code, and any reward amounts in the hackathon are examples. The letter must not claim knowledge of the recipient’s commute unless suitable authorised observations exist.

## First-use journey and jobs

```text
Council letter
      |
      v
Activate invitation
      |
      v
Confirm linked vehicle and complete verification
      |
      v
Meet your FlexZone
      |
      v
Recognise your routine
      |
      v
Find your flex -> Accept an offer -> See outcome and reward
```

| Moment | Person’s job to be done |
| --- | --- |
| Letter | Understand who is inviting me, why, and what participation offers |
| Activation | Join voluntarily through an entry point I can recognise and trust |
| Vehicle confirmation | Confirm the correct vehicle and my right to enrol it |
| FlexZone introduction | Understand exactly which road section and offer window apply |
| Routine playback | Recognise and correct the travel pattern, with its source explained |
| Options and offer | Find a worthwhile change that fits my responsibilities |
| Outcome | Understand whether the condition was met and the reward status |

## Verification and eligibility boundaries

- A code delivered to an owner’s registered address establishes access to that invitation, not conclusively the activator’s identity.
- Identity and owner-authorisation checks must be resolved by the pilot’s verification design. Another household member may receive the letter.
- Council access to owner/address records for this purpose is not established. A permitted access arrangement with an appropriate records partner is required.
- Owner records, personal travel observations, and reward eligibility are separate dependencies.
- Invitation activation must not reveal personal vehicle history before the required verification and authorisation are complete.
- A linked vehicle is not automatically eligible for every reward. Relevant peak use and additionality still need assessment.
- Include expired, already-used, wrong-vehicle, and authorised-driver routes without exposing another participant’s details.

## Hackathon scope

Prototype the letter-to-app transition using a synthetic invitation, example vehicle, and simulated activation/verification. Continue into the FlexZone, recent road history, feasible alternatives, and one offer. No letter is actually sent and no council or government integration is represented as live.

## Evidence reference

It does not establish permission for the proposed FlexPay invitation scheme.
