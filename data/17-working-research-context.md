# FlexPay Government Pilot Viability Working Document

Repository snapshot: 4 October 2026. Source: [live working document](https://chatgpt.com/space/page_d1ddfeefe73081918b7446d988fc329f).

Working research record for Alist, Grace and Tiernan, started on 4 October 2026. This document assesses whether government should fund FlexPay, a voluntary reward for additional car journeys avoided at a specific congested road and time. It consolidates the discussion and provides a queue for continuing research.

The current assessment is that a bounded pilot is plausible. Continuing public funding needs measured additional behaviour change, a demonstrable traffic benefit, a lawful verification and reward process, and benefits that justify the full cost. No measured capacity or causal traffic result has yet been established for our candidate N7 location. The preferred current N7 planning example is the four-hour scenario below; the earlier 45-minute cost and time-value screens remain historical illustrations.

## Four-hour N7 planning example

Preferred planning scenario as of 4 October 2026: investigate approximately 1,600 additional car arrivals avoided each weekday across a 06:00 to 10:00 inbound window at the Newlands Cross to Red Cow/M50 approach. This is a conditional planning model, not a measured N7 requirement or evidence that EUR 3 will attract enough drivers. The original 135-arrival, 45-minute calculations are retained below as a separate small illustration and must not be used to price this larger scenario.

TII's 2025 Network Indicators, inspected directly including the map and colour chart, report annual average daily traffic of 115,750 vehicles and an 8.3 percent heavy-goods share on the N7 between the M50 and Newlands Cross (page 28). These are all-day, both-direction figures. Page 30 classifies the towards-M50 N7 segment as Level of Service F, forced or breakdown flow, in the 06:00, 07:00, 08:00 and 09:00 hourly bins on a typical working day. This supports investigating a broader morning window than the original 45 minutes; it does not establish four-hour inbound volumes, current capacity, a turning movement's constraint or the precise start and end of queues on a particular day. [TII 2025 Network Indicators, pages 28 and 30](https://www.tii.ie/media/w4xns2ed/tii-national-road-and-greenway-network-indicators-2025.pdf).

Define the bottleneck before treating aggregate N7 removals as useful. A queue into a particular M50 slip road may respond chiefly to changes in that movement; rewards to vehicles going elsewhere may offer little relief. Determine whether the constraint is local weaving, a slip road or downstream M50 spillback. Keep the detector screenline and rewarded movement consistent.

Illustrative model inputs: one inbound screenline, a constant effective bottleneck capacity of 4,000 vehicles per hour, and a desired arrival ceiling of 3,800 per hour to leave 5 percent spare capacity. The four hourly demand assumptions below average 4,200 per hour, rounded from the starter example. Neither demand nor capacity has been measured for this location. The hourly shape is synthetic and requires calibration with recent 15-minute observations.

| Morning arrival window | Assumed arrivals without FlexPay | Additional cars to avoid | Arrivals after reduction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 06:00 to 07:00 | 4,100 | 300 | 3,800 |
| 07:00 to 08:00 | 4,400 | 600 | 3,800 |
| 08:00 to 09:00 | 4,300 | 500 | 3,800 |
| 09:00 to 10:00 | 4,000 | 200 | 3,800 |
| Total over four hours | 16,800 | 1,600 | 15,200 |

The reduction is 9.52 percent of assumed incoming vehicles, averaging 400 cars per hour but concentrated in the busiest periods. One person representing one separate car normally supplies one avoided morning arrival. Their return journey does not halve the morning requirement. Actual vehicle classes and effective capacity must account for the traffic mix; this table does not assert a measured N7 car-only flow.

Target the specific arrival periods, preferably in 15-minute intervals. Under the synthetic uniform-within-hour assumptions, the reduction per 15 minutes is respectively 75, 150, 125 and 50 cars across the four hours. Removing 1,600 cars predominantly after the busy period would not reproduce the model's benefit. Shifting travel before 06:00 or after 10:00 requires measured spare capacity there. Remote work, suitable public transport and car sharing can also avoid arrivals; verify net vehicle change and alternatives.

To merely match the assumed 4,000-per-hour capacity requires 100, 400, 300 and zero removals in the four hours, totalling 800. Another 800 removals provide the 200-per-hour headroom. This is why 1,600 is a more robust planning target than stopping exactly at the threshold. Five percent headroom is a design assumption, not an accepted N7 safety margin or a guarantee against incidents.

An elementary queue model starts with no queue at 06:00 and updates the queue by arrivals minus discharge capacity, with a floor of zero. Without the intervention it gives queue sizes of 100, 500, 800 and 800 vehicles at the four hourly ends. An 800-vehicle queue discharging at 4,000 per hour implies up to about 12 minutes of waiting in this simplified model. Keeping arrivals at 3,800 prevents this particular queue from building under the assumptions. This is not a forecast of N7 journey-time savings: changing capacity, existing queues, variable arrivals, interacting lanes, alternative routes and downstream restrictions require calibration or traffic simulation. [FHWA bottleneck concepts](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/operations/16064/004.cfm).

Capacity uncertainty changes the target substantially. Holding the synthetic demand profile and the 5 percent margin fixed, effective capacities of 3,800, 4,000, 4,200 and 4,400 per hour imply 2,360, 1,600, 840 and 340 removals respectively. These are sensitivity cases, not confidence limits. The 2012 Dublin lane-capacity study provides useful methods and cautions about downstream restrictions, but its surveyed radial sites do not include the N7 and its generic lane values do not establish today's N7 capacity. [TII lane-capacity study, tables 4.2 and 5.2 and conclusions](https://www.tii.ie/media/5ibeml3l/a-study-of-lane-capacity-in-the-greater-dublin-area.pdf).

### Participation and rewards for the larger scenario

Assume verified payment at EUR 3 total per qualifying participant day, covering outward and return conditions. Additionality is the proportion of paid outcomes that occurred because of FlexPay; compliance is separately the proportion of commitments that qualify. Both remain unmeasured.

| Additionality assumption | Paid participant-days each weekday | Rewards per weekday | Rewards over 50 weekdays | Rewards over 220 weekdays |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100 percent | 1,600 | EUR 4,800 | EUR 240,000 | EUR 1,056,000 |
| 80 percent | 2,000 | EUR 6,000 | EUR 300,000 | EUR 1,320,000 |
| 60 percent | 2,667 | EUR 8,001 | EUR 400,050 | EUR 1,760,220 |

The 80 percent row is a budgeting sensitivity, not an evidence-backed forecast. It assigns 375, 750, 625 and 250 paid outcomes to the four morning windows. At 90 percent completion, rounding commitments within each hourly cohort gives 417, 834, 695 and 278 acceptances, or 2,224 in total. These commitments create maximum daily reward liability of EUR 6,672 if all qualify, versus approximately EUR 6,000 expected at the assumed completion rate. Expected additional removals are not guaranteed; required extra reserve depends on day-to-day and correlated variability.

Registrations are not daily completions. With a separately assumed 60 percent daily availability, 90 percent completion and 80 percent additionality, the roster required for 1,600 expected additional removals is about 3,704 regular drivers, suggesting an initial recruitment planning allowance of roughly 4,000. Availability across the correct hourly and junction-movement cohorts must be demonstrated. This allowance is not evidence that enough commuters will respond to EUR 3.

Fifty weekdays represent ten reward weeks. The EUR 300,000 central reward pool excludes setup, legal work, access to data, verification, recruitment, payments, participant support, fraud prevention, operation and independent evaluation. Supplier estimates are still required. A EUR 50,000 reward pool at the same price and additionality buys about 13,333 additional avoided arrivals in total, equivalent to 267 per day across 50 days, before any delivery costs. It cannot fund the 1,600-per-day target for ten weeks. A smaller pilot can first estimate behaviour and verification costs, but may be too small to demonstrate a corridor traffic improvement.

### Proposed actionable result and evaluation

Use a preliminary success brief of approximately 1,600 causal morning removals spread across the required windows, plus a measured improvement such as three minutes in average journey time on the defined corridor or movement, accompanied by improved reliability and no material increase in delays on nearby roads or adjacent hours. These are proposed decision criteria, not results or thresholds validated by TII. Baseline delay and variability must establish whether three minutes is attainable and how many trial days are needed.

Three minutes for the 15,200 remaining vehicles, assuming one commuter per vehicle and the earlier approximate EUR 15.58 hourly screening value, equals 760 hours and about EUR 11,841 per weekday in time benefits. That is economic value, not government income. It is conditional on the average saving actually being measured across that population; the elementary queue model does not demonstrate it. Retain participant inconvenience, alternative journeys, delivery costs and proper Irish appraisal treatment. Do not add automatic carbon, safety or evening benefits.

Collect at least several representative school-term weeks of baseline data to establish the profile and variability; four weeks is an initial planning allowance, not a power calculation. Obtain upstream arrivals, turning movements, bottleneck discharge, speeds and corridor journey-time distributions at 15-minute resolution, with nearby roads, shoulder hours, incidents, roadworks and weather. A controlled comparison is needed to separate incentive effects from normal changes. Observed flow after a congested bottleneck is not automatically incoming demand, and improved discharge can increase even when arrivals decrease.

Before approving a larger trial, answer three decisions: does a measured traffic model show that this scale of removals will produce a worthwhile improvement; can a lawful, affordable verification process demonstrate sufficient additional behaviour change at EUR 3; and does the full public appraisal justify the complete budget? The next data work should calibrate this scenario rather than treating its numbers as established facts.

## Public value and original 45-minute screening

Use two separate assessments. Public value includes time, reliability, emissions, collision risk and net transport operating costs. Exchequer cash flows include programme spending, any demonstrable public service savings and changes in tax or applicable toll receipts. Reward payments are transfers within the national economy; a formal appraisal must avoid treating the same transfer as both an additional resource cost and a new public benefit.

For preliminary screening, TII's September 2026 parameters give base commuting time at EUR 8.90 per hour in 2016 prices. Applying annual growth factors for 2017 to 2026 gives EUR 11.987 in 2016 prices. An approximate 1.30 CPI adjustment gives EUR 15.58 in current money. The 2026 carbon value of EUR 346.09 per tonne in 2016 prices similarly becomes about EUR 450 per tonne. These are derived screening values, not a substitute for a formal appraisal in a consistent price base. [TII current parameters](https://cdn.tii.ie/publications/PE-PAG-02030-08.pdf), [CSO August 2026 CPI](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-cpi/consumerpriceindexaugust2026/).

Assume for simplicity one commuter in each of the 3,000 remaining vehicles:

| Average morning time saving | Daily hours saved | Approximate daily benefit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 minute | 50 | EUR 780 |
| 2 minutes | 100 | EUR 1,560 |
| 5 minutes | 250 | EUR 3,900 |

The EUR 50,000 and EUR 100,000 pilot scenarios average EUR 1,000 and EUR 2,000 per reward day. Time alone matches those cash budgets at about 1.3 and 2.6 minutes per remaining driver. Raising the screening hurdle using TII's 1.3 public-funds factor gives approximately 1.7 and 3.3 minutes. This conservative budget screen is not a formal benefit cost ratio.

Include participant inconvenience, slower alternative journeys and displaced queues. Business, freight and bus users require appropriate occupancy and time values. Reliability needs its own observed distribution. Do not add a separate productivity benefit that duplicates valued business time.

## Potential savings by car reduction

Assessment dated 4 October 2026. This benefit register identifies what FlexPay could improve and the evidence needed to value it. A percentage reduction in cars at the morning bottleneck is the scenario input; it is not the percentage reduction in every cost. Distinguish fewer peak car arrivals, fewer daily car kilometres and a sustained reduction in car dependence. Record benefits to society separately from cash released in public budgets.

### Areas where benefits could arise

| Area | Potential benefit | How it relates to fewer cars |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Travel time | Less queuing for people who continue travelling | Measure total person hours saved across affected routes and periods; counts alone do not establish minutes saved. |
| Reliability | More predictable arrivals and less time allowed for delays | Measure the journey time distribution; avoid counting the same time improvement twice. |
| Freight and business | Less delivery delay and time spent travelling for work | Include in the appropriate business time calculation, rather than adding generic productivity savings. |
| Public transport | Better bus running times and potentially lower operating requirements | Only where services share affected congestion; more passengers may require extra capacity. |
| Fuel and electricity | Less energy consumed by avoided driving or changed traffic conditions | Use vehicle kilometres, fleet mix and speed profiles, then subtract replacement travel energy. |
| Vehicle wear | Lower variable tyre, brake and servicing costs | Primarily driven by kilometres and operating conditions; a short pilot does not remove ownership or insurance costs. |
| Climate and ESG environment | Lower greenhouse gas emissions | Model net emissions across modes and time periods; retimed journeys still involve driving. |
| Local air quality | Reduced exhaust and tyre or brake pollution exposure | Model fleet, speeds, location and exposed population; do not apply a traffic percentage to health outcomes. |
| Noise and wellbeing | Lower exposure to traffic noise and disturbance | Assess noise and population exposure by time of day; shifting traffic earlier can move the disturbance. |
| Collisions and emergency response | Potentially lower casualty, damage and incident response costs | Requires changes in exposure, speeds, road type and severity; no proportional crash reduction is established. |
| Active travel and ESG social | Physical activity benefits where people replace driving with safe walking or cycling | Depends on actual mode change and additional activity, rather than the number of peak cars alone. |
| Road maintenance | Possible change in pavement deterioration and intervention timing | Requires axle loading and asset condition evidence; fewer peak cars alone do not establish budget savings. |
| Future infrastructure | Potential postponement or avoidance of capacity works | Requires a sustained demand change and a project whose expenditure would actually change. |
| Parking and land | Reduced parking demand and possible released space | Requires lower car use at destinations; changing departure time may leave daily parking demand intact. |
| Accessibility and public realm | Potentially better access to jobs, services and safer or more usable local streets | Assess who benefits and who faces extra travel costs; an N7 change does not itself establish a local street benefit. |
| Wider resource effects | Possible reduced imported energy, materials and construction impacts | Report where relevant, without adding the same fuel or infrastructure resource saving twice. |
| ESG governance | Auditable delivery, privacy, fair eligibility and reliable reporting | Treat as programme requirements and performance measures; do not attach an automatic separate euro saving. |

These are proposed assessment areas, not demonstrated N7 outcomes. TII's [CBA guidance, December 2023](https://www.tiipublications.ie/library/PE-PAG-02020-02.pdf), identifies appraisal categories and distinguishes user benefits from Exchequer cash flows. Its vehicle operating cost assessment uses both distance and speed; operating cost changes can have either sign.

Emissions need the fleet and speed treatment in the [TII Road Emissions Model development report, May 2024](https://cdn.tii.ie/publications/GE-ENV-01107-02.pdf). TII's [pavement design standard, August 2022, section 2.3](https://cdn.tii.ie/publications/DN-PAV-03021-06.pdf), uses standard axle loading and defines the design lane by commercial vehicle traffic. Our inference is that a percentage fall in passenger cars cannot justify the same percentage reduction in maintenance spending.

The [RSA safe speeds evidence](https://www.rsa.ie/road-safety/research/reports/safe-speeds) makes speed relevant to collision risk and harm. Keep the initial quantified safety benefit at zero until a supported appraisal model is available. [EPA noise and health guidance](https://www.epa.ie/environment-and-you/noise/noise-and-your-health/) supports assessing disturbance and health exposure. [WHO's 7 June 2022 evidence review](https://www.who.int/europe/news/item/07-06-2022-cycling-and-walking-can-help-reduce-physical-inactivity-and-air-pollution--save-lives-and-mitigate-climate-change) supports active travel health benefits, conditional on suitable, safe mode change.

### Worked example at 10 percent fewer morning cars

At about 1,243 fewer car arrivals, three different behaviours imply different benefits. If all journeys move outside the morning window, no daily driving distance is eliminated by that change alone. If all 1,243 changes eliminate an assumed 50 km daily round trip, gross removed car distance is 62,150 km per day. If only 30% eliminate that round trip and the rest simply retime, gross removed distance is 18,645 km per day. These are scenario calculations, not observed participant behaviour.

Using an illustrative 0.18 kg CO2e per car km gives 11.19 tonnes per day for the all eliminated case or 3.36 tonnes for the 30% case. The factor and distance are unverified planning assumptions. These are gross removed car emissions, not net programme emissions: account for replacement transport, rebound and changed emissions from remaining traffic. Do not describe them as carbon credit income.

Time benefits need affected person journeys multiplied by measured minutes saved, divided by 60, and valued by journey purpose. Apply the appropriate appraisal treatment to changed demand and include participant inconvenience. Do not infer that a 10% car reduction means a 10% reduction in delay, emissions, crashes or expenditure.

At the assumed EUR 3 total daily reward and an assumed 80% additionality per rewarded person day, roughly 1,554 rewarded person days would purchase 1,243 additional changed morning arrivals: EUR 4,662 per day before delivery and verification. This assumes compliance and one relevant morning car arrival per additional participant. Additionality means the change happened because of the programme; the 80% value is a sensitivity assumption, not a trial result.

### Government and societal accounting

The societal account should combine supported time, operating cost, climate, safety and other benefits, deduct resource and participant costs, and avoid overlap between freight time, general productivity, noise health and collision cost components. Rewards are public expenditure and transfers to participants, not newly created social value; apply the required treatment of public funds and taxes.

The public cash account should record reward and delivery costs, any demonstrable reduction in maintenance, health or other budgets, actual project expenditure changes and changes in tax or public revenue. Monetised time and carbon benefits do not pay cash into the Exchequer. Reduced fuel purchases may also lower tax receipts. Deferred projects are timing changes unless spending is actually avoided. Existing road spending is not a proportional pool of savings.

Proposed initial modelling priority: measure time and reliability, then net operating costs and emissions. Keep maintenance, collision and deferred capital values unmonetised until the required evidence supports them. Next unresolved question: what speed and journey time improvement would each reduction scenario cause across the N7 corridor and neighbouring routes?

## Emissions safety health and network effects

A shifted departure can reduce peak demand without eliminating driving. Emissions depend on changes in distance, speed, fuel, vehicle fleet and replacement travel. Work from home or a genuinely eliminated car journey can produce a clearer distance reduction.

Historical 45-minute scenario only, not the current four-hour planning case: if the 135 additional changes each eliminate 50 km of daily driving and net emissions fall by an assumed 0.18 kg per km, the reduction is 1.215 tonnes per day. At the approximate appraisal value above, this is about EUR 547 per day in climate benefits. The distance and emission factor are unverified assumptions. Replacement travel, rebound trips and relevant additional energy must be accounted for. This value is not carbon credit income or an automatic saving in government expenditure.

Collision benefits require changes in vehicle kilometres, road classes, speeds and severity risks. Do not infer that 4.3 percent fewer arrivals means 4.3 percent fewer collisions. Use zero safety benefit in the initial screening base case until a defensible model is available. A short pilot cannot establish a reduction in rare serious collisions by simple before and after counting. [TII CBA collision and emissions guidance](https://www.tiipublications.ie/library/PE-PAG-02020-02.pdf)

Other assessment items are local air pollution, walking and cycling health benefits where relevant, net fuel and vehicle operating costs, public transport capacity and cost, travel on neighbouring roads, adjacent peak windows and whether future infrastructure expenditure is genuinely deferred. Use a single accounting approach to prevent double counting.

### Number plate access

TII's public privacy notice confirms encrypted plate records on an ANPR network that includes the N7, collected to determine journey times. It does not establish an open plate feed, FlexPay-specific reuse permission, a matching API or the exact ANPR camera positions at our candidate bottleneck. Its public notice gives a purpose-based retention rule rather than a fixed ANPR day limit. [TII data protection notice](https://www.tii.ie/en/compliance/data-protection-notice/).

We would link a registered plate and its observations to a participant, so this must be treated as personal data. Replacing the plate with a hash or participant token reduces exposure but normally leaves the records pseudonymised and subject to GDPR while they can be linked back. A plate being visible on a public road does not establish unrestricted reuse rights. [DPC personal-data guidance](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/dpc-guidance/what-is-personal-data), [DPC pseudonymisation guidance](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/dpc-guidance/anonymisation-pseudonymisation).

| Access route | Preliminary assessment |
|---|---|
| Government sponsor working with TII and its operator | Preferred route to investigate. Ask whether the agency can lawfully provide aggregate performance data and a limited participant-matching service. Need a purpose and legal-basis assessment, technical feasibility, controller roles, applicable agreements and costs. No service is confirmed. |
| A specifically authorised ANPR provider for an opted-in pilot | Possible route to assess. Participants register a relevant vehicle, but their participation does not authorise capturing every other road user. Camera placement, lawful collection, coverage, notices, nonparticipant processing and retention need assessment. |
| Prototype without ANPR | Aggregate modelling and synthetic participant/reward records can proceed now. A later limited verification trial could assess declared travel shifts or other evidence; phone location, employer and transport-account data also require their own privacy and access assessment. |

A commercial contract or a data-sharing agreement does not itself settle the GDPR legal basis. The responsible controller must identify the purpose and necessary processing. A public authority cannot use legitimate interests for processing in performance of its public tasks; consent needs a real, free choice and is not a general substitute for statutory authority. Voluntary enrolment also does not settle the lawful reuse of an existing journey-time network. The government sponsor's DPO and legal team should assess the actual arrangement and safeguards. [DPC legal-basis guidance, December 2019](https://www.dataprotection.ie/sites/default/files/uploads/2020-04/Guidance%20on%20Legal%20Bases.pdf), [DPC public-sector sharing guidance](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/dpc-guidance/data-sharing-public-sector).

The proposed partner interface returns a participant token, target window, observation state of seen/not_seen/unknown, coverage state, policy version and a restricted evidence reference where needed. Plate registration and raw matching would remain in a separate controlled environment; operational FlexPay would receive the minimum justified result. These results are still personal data. The JSON schema is a proposed shape only; authentication, integrity, retention and error policies need implementation and approval. The exact cryptographic matching method must be agreed with the provider, not assumed from TII's word encrypted.

Even a functioning ANPR service observes a vehicle, not necessarily the enrolled driver. No detection can reflect missed reads, a different lane or route, a borrowed/replacement vehicle or an outage. Unknown or inadequate coverage must not count as verified avoidance. A seen vehicle also needs the agreed household/shared-vehicle rule before a participant is penalised. Registering a plate does not require us to identify every road user's owner. Any separate vehicle-owner database access would be a different dependency, not something acquired through the public camera.

### Retention and operational safeguards

There is no universal 28-day allowance for FlexPay to keep plates. Retention must be necessary for a stated purpose. The DPC's CCTV guidance discusses about one month in an ordinary security/personal-injury context; that is not a licence for a traffic incentive scheme. Set separate justified periods for plate registration, raw reads/images, verification and appeals, reward accounting and anonymised evaluation. Preserve only the evidence needed for a genuine unresolved dispute, with a defined hold and deletion trigger. Longer accounting obligations do not automatically require longer retention of plate movements. [DPC CCTV guidance, November 2023, page 14](https://www.dataprotection.ie/sites/default/files/uploads/2025-01/CCTV%20Guidance%20Data%20Controllers_November%202023%20EN.pdf).

Treat a DPIA as an initial requirement to resolve before a real ANPR pilot. It is legally mandatory for likely high-risk processing, including systematic monitoring of publicly accessible areas on a large scale. A limited result interface is a mitigation, not an exemption. Establish who determines purposes and who processes on whose behalf, appropriate controller/processor or joint-controller arrangements, restricted access and audit logs, security and deletion controls, clear participant information, and human review of contested reads. [DPC DPIA guidance](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/organisations/know-your-obligations/data-protection-impact-assessments), [DPC controller and processor guidance](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/organisations/know-your-obligations/controller-and-processor-relationships).

## Comparable initiatives

### Singapore reward programmes

INSINC means Incentives for Singapore Commuters. The January 2012 rail trial was run by Stanford University and the National University of Singapore, supported by Singapore’s Land Transport Authority (LTA) and SMRT. Commuters registered their fare-card identifier; recorded journeys earned credits, with bonuses for off-peak travel. They could exchange credits at a fixed rate or enter an online cash-prize game. Referral rewards, friend rankings and personalised weekly “Magic Box” offers supported recruitment and engagement. This is the relevant historical comparator for FlexPay’s proposed lottery option. [Singapore Civil Service College, ETHOS June 2013, pp. 48–50](https://file.go.gov.sg/ethos-issue-12.pdf)

The initial six-month evaluation reported 20,319 admitted participants and a 7.49 percent relative reduction in participants’ peak-trip share. This was a comparison before and after joining, without a reported randomised control group; it did not establish an equivalent reduction in total rail demand. Participants chose their reward method, so differences between game users and fixed-reward users do not establish that a lottery caused a larger shift. [Original evaluation, JOURNEYS September 2013, pp. 34–36](https://web.stanford.edu/~balaji/papers/13INSINC.pdf)

LTA subsequently documented Travel Smart Rewards in its 2014/15 annual report. Its current programme is Travel Smart Journeys (TSJ), delivered through SimplyGo, with fare rebates rather than the historical INSINC prize game. Registration links a nominated travel card to eligible journeys; points can be redeemed in 500-point blocks worth S$5, credited to the card within three calendar weeks. [LTA annual report, pp. 42–43](https://www.lta.gov.sg/content/dam/ltagov/who_we_are/statistics_and_publications/report/pdf/LTA_AR_14-15_RGB.pdf), [current SimplyGo programme](https://www.simplygo.com.sg/travel-smart-journeys)

Since 27 December 2025, TSJ has offered 80 percent rebates for eligible express-bus journeys and qualifying connecting buses around free off-peak rail rides. The separate free-rail scheme covers journeys starting at six North East Line stations between Punggol Coast and Kovan, or Sengkang/Punggol LRT stations, before 07:30 or from 09:00 to 09:45 on weekdays excluding public holidays. LTA’s 4 March 2026 release reported approximately 8 percent shifting out of the morning peak following the combined measures, alongside network and service improvements. This is an agency-reported outcome for the package, not an isolated lottery effect or an N7 forecast. [LTA March 2026 release](https://www.lta.gov.sg/content/ltagov/en/newsroom/2026/3/news-releases/8-percent-nel-commuters-shift-out-morning-peak-travel.html)

Implication for Ireland: fare-card records can establish a qualifying public-transport journey. They cannot by themselves prove that the participant would otherwise have driven through the N7 bottleneck. FlexPay still needs lawful verification, additionality measurement and workable alternatives or work-time flexibility. Singapore supports testing voluntary incentives; it does not settle Irish lottery legality, Leap integration, delivery costs or government value.

The early Dutch Spitsmijden experiment used EUR 3 to EUR 7 rewards and reported a large reduction in participating drivers' peak journeys, mainly through time shifts. The participant result is not an equivalent reduction in total road traffic, and the original trial was too small to establish network congestion benefits. Peak use increased after incentives ended. [Original research](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/48319986_Using_incentives_as_traffic_management_tool_empirical_results_of_the_peak_avoidance_experiment)

Stanford CAPRI combined incentives with other programme features and used boundary RFID detection. Participants could redeem points through fixed or random rewards; high use of random redemption does not establish that a lottery produces more additional avoided trips at equal cost. [CAPRI paper](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/trb2015-3.pdf)

Transferability questions include workplace flexibility, care responsibilities, available transport, existing peak congestion, trust, sensor reliability and legal access. Treat national culture explanations as hypotheses requiring evidence.

## Reward law and Leap credits

A guaranteed reward for a verified outcome is the simpler working option for lottery-law analysis. The proposed lottery still needs current Irish classification and an appropriate lawful route before a live draw.

The eISB commencement table checked in this discussion showed sections 91 to 93 of the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 as not commenced. Section 93 therefore must not be presented as an operative exemption. GRAI indicates that existing lottery permit and licence arrangements continue during phased licensing. The product-marketing exemption in section 27A of the revised 1956 Act has conditions, including a EUR 2,500 prize limit and no additional entry or redemption charge; FlexPay has not been shown to qualify. Free entry or a valuable transport prize does not itself settle classification. [Commencement table](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/isbc/2024_35.html), [GRAI licensing phasing](https://www.grai.ie/licensing-regulation/business-to-consumer-licenses/licensing-phasing), [Existing section 27A](https://revisedacts.lawreform.ie/eli/1956/act/2/section/27A/revised/en/html).

Leap consumer top-ups have a EUR 5 minimum, and refunds are possible under applicable terms. No bulk reward integration, discounted issuance or authorised credit API has been verified. Credits may suit transit users but are less useful to some people working from home, cycling or shifting departure. Offer equal-value choice only if a delivery mechanism is established. Credits should not be presumed cheaper than cash or outside prize regulation. [Leap terms](https://about.leapcard.ie/terms-and-conditions), [Leap Top-Up app](https://about.leapcard.ie/leap-top-up-app).

A lottery at EUR 3 expected reward per qualifying participant day has the same expected reward pool as a fixed EUR 3 payment. Savings require demonstrated additional behaviour change per euro. Any lottery experiment needs transparent odds, capped liability and a lawful design.

## People and service experience

The authority needs to decide where and when intervention is justified, control the budget and judge additional public benefit. Participants need understandable eligibility, a voluntary choice, clear reward conditions, accessible verification and a way to resolve errors. People who cannot change travel should benefit from improved reliability and should be considered in the fairness assessment.

The central opportunity is to improve reliable travel through a constrained road section by enabling feasible voluntary changes. Supporting opportunity statements include helping government make decisions from trustworthy data, protecting privacy, demonstrating value for money and preventing congestion from moving to other places or times.
