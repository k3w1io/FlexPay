# FlexPay potential savings by car reduction

Assessment dated 4 October 2026. This benefit register identifies what FlexPay could improve and the evidence needed to value it. A percentage reduction in cars at the morning bottleneck is the scenario input; it is not the percentage reduction in every cost. Distinguish fewer peak car arrivals, fewer daily car kilometres and a sustained reduction in car dependence. Record benefits to society separately from cash released in public budgets.

### Areas where benefits could arise

| Area | Potential benefit | How it relates to fewer cars |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Travel time | Less queuing for people who continue travelling | Measure total person hours saved across affected routes and periods; counts alone do not establish minutes saved. |
| Reliability | More predictable arrivals and less time allowed for delays | Measure the journey time distribution; avoid counting the same time improvement twice. |
| Freight and business | Less delivery delay and time spent travelling for work | Include in the appropriate business time calculation, rather than adding generic productivity savings. |
| Public transport | Better bus running times and potentially lower operating requirements | Only where services share affected congestion; more passengers may require extra capacity. |
| Fuel and electricity | Less energy consumed by avoided driving or changed traffic conditions | Use vehicle kilometres, fleet mix and speed profiles, then subtract replacement travel energy. |
| Vehicle wear | Lower variable tyre, brake and servicing costs | Primarily driven by kilometres and operating conditions; a short pilot does not remove ownership or insurance costs. |
| Climate and ESG environment | Lower greenhouse gas emissions | Model net emissions across modes and time periods; retimed journeys still involve driving. |
| Local air quality | Reduced exhaust and tyre or brake pollution exposure | Model fleet, speeds, location and exposed population; do not apply a traffic percentage to health outcomes. |
| Noise and wellbeing | Lower exposure to traffic noise and disturbance | Assess noise and population exposure by time of day; shifting traffic earlier can move the disturbance. |
| Collisions and emergency response | Potentially lower casualty, damage and incident response costs | Requires changes in exposure, speeds, road type and severity; no proportional crash reduction is established. |
| Active travel and ESG social | Physical activity benefits where people replace driving with safe walking or cycling | Depends on actual mode change and additional activity, rather than the number of peak cars alone. |
| Road maintenance | Possible change in pavement deterioration and intervention timing | Requires axle loading and asset condition evidence; fewer peak cars alone do not establish budget savings. |
| Future infrastructure | Potential postponement or avoidance of capacity works | Requires a sustained demand change and a project whose expenditure would actually change. |
| Parking and land | Reduced parking demand and possible released space | Requires lower car use at destinations; changing departure time may leave daily parking demand intact. |
| Accessibility and public realm | Potentially better access to jobs, services and safer or more usable local streets | Assess who benefits and who faces extra travel costs; an N7 change does not itself establish a local street benefit. |
| Wider resource effects | Possible reduced imported energy, materials and construction impacts | Report where relevant, without adding the same fuel or infrastructure resource saving twice. |
| ESG governance | Auditable delivery, privacy, fair eligibility and reliable reporting | Treat as programme requirements and performance measures; do not attach an automatic separate euro saving. |

These are proposed assessment areas, not demonstrated N7 outcomes. TII's [CBA guidance, December 2023](https://www.tiipublications.ie/library/PE-PAG-02020-02.pdf), identifies appraisal categories and distinguishes user benefits from Exchequer cash flows. Its vehicle operating cost assessment uses both distance and speed; operating cost changes can have either sign.

Emissions need the fleet and speed treatment in the [TII Road Emissions Model development report, May 2024](https://cdn.tii.ie/publications/GE-ENV-01107-02.pdf). TII's [pavement design standard, August 2022, section 2.3](https://cdn.tii.ie/publications/DN-PAV-03021-06.pdf), uses standard axle loading and defines the design lane by commercial vehicle traffic. Our inference is that a percentage fall in passenger cars cannot justify the same percentage reduction in maintenance spending.

The [RSA safe speeds evidence](https://www.rsa.ie/road-safety/research/reports/safe-speeds) makes speed relevant to collision risk and harm. Keep the initial quantified safety benefit at zero until a supported appraisal model is available. [EPA noise and health guidance](https://www.epa.ie/environment-and-you/noise/noise-and-your-health/) supports assessing disturbance and health exposure. [WHO's 7 June 2022 evidence review](https://www.who.int/europe/news/item/07-06-2022-cycling-and-walking-can-help-reduce-physical-inactivity-and-air-pollution--save-lives-and-mitigate-climate-change) supports active travel health benefits, conditional on suitable, safe mode change.

### N7 percentage scenarios

The preserved TII report for 1 October 2026 records 12,426 CAR passages and 17,821 Any passages eastbound at site 000000001070 between 06:00 and 10:00, on the source clock. CAR is included in Any. Use this single morning to explain scale, not as a calibrated multiweek baseline, capacity estimate or intervention target. See the [official CAR report](https://trafficdata.tii.ie/tfdayreport.asp?sgid=XzOA8m4lr27P0HaO3_srSB&spid=ABEB430EAE98&reportdate=2026-10-01&enddate=2026-10-01&dir=%2D1&intval=2&dim1bin=2). The underlying aggregate records are preserved in the research data pack.

| Fewer morning cars | Fewer car passages from 06:00 to 10:00 | Reduction in all vehicle passages | Gross daily car km avoided if each change eliminates 50 km |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2% | 249 | 1.4% | 12,450 |
| 5% | 621 | 3.5% | 31,050 |
| 10% | 1,243 | 7.0% | 62,150 |
| 15% | 1,864 | 10.5% | 93,200 |
| 20% | 2,485 | 13.9% | 124,250 |

Calculation assumptions: car passages are rounded to the nearest whole vehicle; other vehicle classes are unchanged for the all vehicle percentage. The final column assumes one additional changed morning car arrival per eliminated daily 50 km round trip. This is a gross car distance illustration, before replacement travel and rebound, and is zero for simple retiming without a change in distance. Car passages are not unique commuters or participants. A fall in counter throughput can also reflect worse congestion; successful demand reduction must be established with performance and displacement evidence.

The earlier 1,600 additional avoided morning arrivals remain a hypothetical planning case. If expressed against this particular observed morning, they equal about 12.9% of CAR passages or 9.0% of Any passages. Neither ratio establishes the reduction needed to improve the N7.

### Worked example at 10 percent fewer morning cars

At about 1,243 fewer car arrivals, three different behaviours imply different benefits. If all journeys move outside the morning window, no daily driving distance is eliminated by that change alone. If all 1,243 changes eliminate an assumed 50 km daily round trip, gross removed car distance is 62,150 km per day. If only 30% eliminate that round trip and the rest simply retime, gross removed distance is 18,645 km per day. These are scenario calculations, not observed participant behaviour.

Using an illustrative 0.18 kg CO2e per car km gives 11.19 tonnes per day for the all eliminated case or 3.36 tonnes for the 30% case. The factor and distance are unverified planning assumptions. These are gross removed car emissions, not net programme emissions: account for replacement transport, rebound and changed emissions from remaining traffic. Do not describe them as carbon credit income.

Time benefits need affected person journeys multiplied by measured minutes saved, divided by 60, and valued by journey purpose. Apply the appropriate appraisal treatment to changed demand and include participant inconvenience. Do not infer that a 10% car reduction means a 10% reduction in delay, emissions, crashes or expenditure.

At the assumed EUR 3 total daily reward and an assumed 80% additionality per rewarded person day, roughly 1,554 rewarded person days would purchase 1,243 additional changed morning arrivals: EUR 4,662 per day before delivery and verification. This assumes compliance and one relevant morning car arrival per additional participant. Additionality means the change happened because of the programme; the 80% value is a sensitivity assumption, not a trial result.

### Government and societal accounting

The societal account should combine supported time, operating cost, climate, safety and other benefits, deduct resource and participant costs, and avoid overlap between freight time, general productivity, noise health and collision cost components. Rewards are public expenditure and transfers to participants, not newly created social value; apply the required treatment of public funds and taxes.

The public cash account should record reward and delivery costs, any demonstrable reduction in maintenance, health or other budgets, actual project expenditure changes and changes in tax or public revenue. Monetised time and carbon benefits do not pay cash into the Exchequer. Reduced fuel purchases may also lower tax receipts. Deferred projects are timing changes unless spending is actually avoided. Existing road spending is not a proportional pool of savings.

Proposed initial modelling priority: measure time and reliability, then net operating costs and emissions. Keep maintenance, collision and deferred capital values unmonetised until the required evidence supports them. Next unresolved question: what speed and journey time improvement would each reduction scenario cause across the N7 corridor and neighbouring routes?
